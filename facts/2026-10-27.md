## Fact
In 1873, Belva Lockwood finished her coursework at DC's National University Law School, but administrators refused to hand her a diploma because she was a woman, blocking her from joining the DC bar. She wrote directly to the school's ex-officio president, Ulysses S. Grant, demanding it—and had her diploma within days.

## Takeaway
A Washington law school's quiet refusal to certify a woman's earned degree shows how much institutional stonewalling women had to out-maneuver just to enter the legal profession.
