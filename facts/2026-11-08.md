# DC Passed Civil Rights Laws in 1872—Then Erased Them

## Fact
In 1872–73, DC's Reconstruction-era government outlawed restaurant segregation, but the laws vanished from the city code around 1901. In 1944, Howard Law student Pauli Murray rediscovered them in old statute books, arming Mary Church Terrell's 1950 Thompson's Restaurant sit-in and a 1953 Supreme Court win.

## Takeaway
DC once had stronger civil-rights protections than most of the country—then quietly buried them for 50 years until a law student's research dug them back up.
